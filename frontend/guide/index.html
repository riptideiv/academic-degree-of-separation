A useful starting point

Discover potential collaborators at your university

If you are starting a project or exploring a research area, a department directory can be a long list of unfamiliar names. Use the authors you already read as a starting point for finding people to investigate.

  1. Add authors whose work interests you

    Open the tool, search a researcher's name, and select the matching profile. Check their affiliation and works to avoid namesakes. Add a few researchers relevant to your question; these become the starting points for Institution Explorer.

  2. Choose your home institution

    Open Institution Explorer, search for your university, and choose it. The explorer looks for researchers at that institution with coauthor paths to your starting authors. Its suggestions use coauthorship, even when the main graph also includes citations or shared institutions.

  3. Read the evidence behind a suggestion

    Look at the research topics, representative works, and publications supporting each coauthor hop. Follow the OpenAlex or ORCID links to check the profile. Affiliation labels distinguish reviewed current evidence from OpenAlex's last-known affiliation.

  4. Build a shortlist, then investigate

    Read a recent paper and check the researcher's university page before deciding whether their work fits your project. A short network path can be a useful discovery clue; it does not establish availability, interest in collaboration, or a personal introduction.

Another way in

Trace a connection between two researchers

Add two researcher profiles to the graph. The sidebar lists degrees of separation and the connecting steps as searches finish. You can also search for a paper by title to explore its authors and citation connections.

For a coauthor network, keep Co-authorship selected and turn off Citations and Institution under Search options. Choose Just the connection for a simpler view, then select Apply options. Mixed edge types answer a broader question about scholarly connections; their distance is not a coauthor-only degree count.

Open the legend to interpret the edges. You can export a graph image or a Markdown path report to keep with your reading notes. If you want a quick introduction, the empty graph includes a Geoffrey Hinton ↔ Yoshua Bengio example.

Two more reasons to explore

Before a conference

Give your reading some context

Add a speaker and an author you follow. Explore connecting work and read it before the session. Use what you learn to prepare a specific research question.

In a classroom

Make network concepts concrete

Compare the same pair with coauthor-only and mixed connections. Discuss how the definition of an edge changes a path, and why data coverage affects what a network can show.

What a connection does—and does not—tell you

The tool uses OpenAlex scholarly records. Author identities, affiliations, and publication coverage can be incomplete or outdated. Check the underlying works and current university pages when a result matters.

Searches have time and coverage limits. Institution Explorer checks a bounded candidate set, not every person at a university. An incomplete search or no path found does not prove that no connection exists. Results are a starting point for research, not an exhaustive ranking of collaborators or a certified Erdős number.
