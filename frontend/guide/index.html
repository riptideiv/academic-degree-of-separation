Discover researchers at your university

  1. Choose your references

    In Discover, select a research topic, paper, or researcher. Confirm the matching record; similar names and titles can refer to different people or work. You can combine up to eight references.

  2. Select a university

    Choose the institution and select Find researchers. Matches use topic and publication evidence from a bounded sample of institution-associated work. You can begin without adding anyone to a graph.

  3. Inspect the evidence

    Read the supporting papers and open a researcher profile. Affiliation labels distinguish reviewed current evidence from OpenAlex's last-known affiliation. Profiles let you switch between recent and most-cited publications.

  4. Save or explore

    Save useful people and papers, or open their network in Connections. Check connection optionally looks for a verified coauthor path of up to two steps to a reference person. No path found does not remove a topical match. Adding graph nodes never changes your discovery references.

Another way in

Trace a connection between two researchers

Open Connections and add two researcher profiles to the graph. The sidebar lists degrees of separation and the connecting steps as searches finish. You can also search for a paper by title to explore its authors and citation connections.

Co-authorship is selected by default. For a coauthor network, keep Co-authorship selected and turn off Citations and Institution under Connection settings. Choose Just the connection for a simpler view, then select Apply options. Mixed edge types answer a broader question about scholarly connections; their distance is not a coauthor-only degree count.

Open the legend to interpret the edges. Select Share map to copy a link that rebuilds your starting points and search settings. Live results may change as records update. You can also export a graph image or a Markdown path report to keep with your reading notes. If you want a quick introduction, the empty graph includes a Geoffrey Hinton ↔ Yoshua Bengio example.

Keep a shortlist

Saved collects researchers and papers in this browser. Notes save as you type. Repeated saves preserve your notes; removing an item offers Undo. There is no account or automatic synchronization.

Export JSON to back up or transfer your list. Exports include notes. Import validates the entire file and previews new items and conflicting notes before you confirm; choose to keep both notes or one version. Lists support up to 300 items and 6,000 characters per note, within a 2 MB file limit.

If browser storage is blocked or full, changes remain available for the visit and a warning prompts you to export them. Private browsing or clearing site data can erase local research. Data does not automatically follow you to another domain: export on the original host and import on the new one.

Share profile and Share search links contain public record IDs, never private notes or the saved list. Discovery links rebuild results against current data. A saved item's Reopen discovery action restores the question that led to it.

What a connection does—and does not—tell you

The tool uses OpenAlex scholarly records. Author identities, affiliations, and publication coverage can be incomplete or outdated. Check the underlying works and current university pages when a result matters.

Searches have time and coverage limits. Discover checks a bounded candidate set, not every person at a university. An incomplete search or no path found does not prove that no connection exists. Results are a starting point for research, not an exhaustive ranking of collaborators or a certified Erdős number.
